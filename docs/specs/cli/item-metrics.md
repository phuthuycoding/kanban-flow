# item-metrics

## Purpose
Give every work item an estimate and a measurement, whether or not the `travel` skill ran it. Estimation is a component of the workflow harness and its data is harness data: the approved contract carries the agent's raw estimate, kf records it at approval, counts the files changed on entering review, measures the item at its close, and learns its calibration from the items it measured. `travel`, the dashboard and later readers only read these figures; none of them estimates, measures or calibrates on its own.

## Current behaviour
- **The contract estimate.** The plan (`phase-2-implementation-plan.md`) and the bug report (`phase-1-spec-requirement.md` of a bug) have an `## Estimate` section holding one line, `- Agent minutes: <n>`: the agent's raw minutes for the whole item, before calibration. kf reads the number after `Agent minutes:`; no section, no number, or a number of 0 or below is no estimate, and the reason says which. For a plan kf also counts the distinct entries of the Task Breakdown `Files` cells: the mark is `exact`, `partial` when a cell is empty or an entry is a glob or a directory (a trailing `/`, or no dot in its last segment), and `unknown` (files `null`) when every cell is empty. A bug's files are always `unknown`.
- **Sizes and points.** S is under 5 minutes and up to 3 files, M up to 20 minutes and 10 files, L up to 60 minutes and 30 files, anything above is XL. The higher of the minutes size and the files size wins; with `unknown` files the size comes from the minutes alone. Points are S 1, M 2, L 4, XL 8.
- **The learned factor.** Per kind (`feature`, `bug`, `pr`), the median of `work ÷ estmin` over the last 30 history rows of that kind that carry estimate minutes, used only from 20 such rows on and clamped to 0.5–3; below 20 rows it is 1. It multiplies the minutes only, never the files, and it learns from the raw estimate and the measured work, never from an earlier factor. A contract estimate is sized from its minutes × the factor of the item's kind against its files.
- **The history.** The measured rows are every `.works/done/<item>/metrics.json` that has an actual size, plus the rows of a legacy `.kf/travel/estimates.md` (the 14-field header ending in `estmin`, or the older 13-field one without it), merged by date — a row's `date`, a measurement's close date — with legacy rows first on the same day. kf only reads `estimates.md`; no command creates, appends to or rewrites it. A legacy row with actual `-`, and a measurement whose actual is missing, are left out. A row without estimate minutes teaches the factor nothing; a measurement with no estimate counts toward budget and throughput but not toward hit rate and bias. A malformed legacy row or `metrics.json` is an error naming the file (and the line).
- **Planning warnings.** While an item is in `planning`, `kf validate`, `kf status --change <feature>` (a `Notices` block, and `notices` in `--json`; `blockers` is unchanged) and `kf approve` report `estimate_missing` (no usable estimate, with the reason) and `estimate_should_split` (the estimate sizes XL; the notice names the minutes, the factor when it is not 1, and the files). Both are WARNINGs and never refuse the approval. No estimate finding is raised outside planning, so items approved earlier pass `kf validate --strict` as before.
- **The history notice.** When the history cannot be read, a planning item's estimate is sized with factor 1 and kf says so: `kf status --change` lists it under `Notices` as `estimate_history`, and `kf approve` prints it (`estimates history: <file:line problem> — factor 1 used`). It is project state, not a finding on the contract, so `kf validate` does not raise it.
- **`meta.estimate`, recorded by `kf approve`.** In the same metadata write as the approval, `kf approve` sets `estimate` in `.kfw.json` to `{ minutes, files, filesMark, size, factor, at }`, or `{ none: true, reason, at }` when the contract has no usable estimate, and the `approved` lifecycle record carries it. Approving the contract approves the estimate; a re-approval writes a new one. When the move after the approval is refused, the previous `estimate` is put back with the previous approval.
- **`meta.filesChanged`, counted on entering review.** Every move into `review` counts, outside the lock, the files the item changed and sets `filesChanged` to `{ count, at }` inside the move, so the `stage` record carries it; a later entry into review overwrites it with a new count. With a worktree, kf runs one `git diff --name-only --no-renames <base>...<branch>`, the base being `origin/<default>`, else the checkout's HEAD; without one, it counts the checkout's current branch against `origin/<default>`. `--no-renames` makes a rename count as two paths whatever the machine's git config. When the count cannot be made — not a git repository, the branch does not exist or cannot be passed to git, no `origin/HEAD` for a checkout without a worktree, a checkout not on a branch, or the diff failing — it records `{ count: null, reason, at }`; the reason names no path or host, and the move is never refused.
- **`<item>/metrics.json`, written at the close.** When kf closes an item from review into `done` (`kf status`, `kf sync`, `kf archive`, `kf stage <feature> done`, `kf issues done`), it writes the measurement after the lock is released and the `delivered` record is written. Reconciling an item already in `done` does not measure it again. The measurement holds `v` (1), `closedAt` (the `delivered` time), `id`, `feature`, `kind`, `estimate` (`meta.estimate`, or `{ none: true, reason }`), `actual` (`{ size, workMinutes }`), `gap` (actual size index minus estimated size index), `stages` (minutes per stage, summed over visits, in the order first entered; backlog is a stage of its own), `path` (the stages in order), `gates` (`requirement` = time in brainstorm, `plan` = time in planning, `merge` = from the review PASS report's `created` stamp, read as local time to the minute and marked `approximate`, to the `delivered` record), `ledger` (fix `rounds`, `seatMinutes`, `models`, and `unparsedLines` when the ledger has lines that do not parse; read from its good lines), `filesChanged`, `tier` (the `Tier and reason` row of the review report) and `loopMetricsRow`. Work minutes run from the first entry into implementation to the review PASS `created` stamp, minus backlog time in between; the merge span is not work. The actual size is `sizeOf(work minutes, files changed)` with the same thresholds.
- **Missing and unreliable figures.** A figure that cannot be taken is `{ missing: <reason> }`, never 0: no lifecycle log or an unreadable line, no implementation stage, no review report or no PASS, no `created` stamp, no tier row, no ledger, no seat lines (`no seat time recorded`), no file count, no estimate. A span whose clock ran backwards is `{ unreliable: "clock went backwards" }`, never a negative number.
- **The loop-metrics row.** The close writes the item's `.works/loop-metrics.md` row, as `kf loop-stats <feature> --record` does, when the ledger is one `--record` accepts, and records `loopMetricsRow: { written: true }`; otherwise, or when the history file refuses the row, `loopMetricsRow` is `{ missing: <reason> }` and no row is written.
- **Nothing machine-local.** The measurement holds no path, host name or worktree; a refusal from the loop history names its file relative to the project.
- **A failed measurement only warns.** Building or writing `metrics.json` is caught after the close: the item stays in `done` with the same exit code and output plus one line, `⚠ warning: the measurement of '<feature>' was not written: <reason>`, printed by `kf archive`, `kf status`, `kf sync`, `kf stage … done` and `kf issues done`. Nothing is rolled back, and in a `kf sync` sweep the other items are still closed and measured.
- **`kf estimate`.** `size <minutes> <files|unknown> [--kind feature|bug|pr]` prints the size, `points: <n>` (with `(should split)` for XL) and `defaults (0 rows measured)`; with `--kind` it reads the history and prints `defaults (<n> <kind> rows with estmin)` or `learned: <m> min × <factor> = <m'> min (<kind>, last <n> rows)`, with `(clamped from <x>)` when clamped. `stats` prints `budget:` (the median of daily actual points once 3 days are measured, else `10 tasks`), `hit rate:`, `bias:`, `throughput:`, `calibration:`, one `kind <k>:` line per kind with its factor, and a `warning:` line when this week's hit rate fell below last week's. `overlap <filesA> <filesB>` prints `sequential` when two plan `Files` cells may touch the same path (an empty cell always may), else `parallel`. `show <feature> [--json]` prints the recorded `meta.estimate`; in brainstorm or planning, the contract estimate marked `not yet approved`; otherwise `no estimate:` and why. These print the lines of the retired travel `estimates` script for the same inputs. Exit 0 printed, 1 any error (bad input, or a history that cannot be read), with the message on stderr after `estimates: `.
- **`kf metrics <feature> [--json]`** prints the item's `metrics.json`, as labelled text (`missing: <reason>` and `unreliable: <reason>` shown as such) or as the JSON itself. Exit 0 printed; 1 no measurement yet, no `.works`, or a `metrics.json` that is not valid JSON or not a JSON object; 3 bad input (no feature, more than one, an unknown feature, a bad option).
- **Travel only reads.** The `travel` skill takes each item's size from `kf estimate show <feature> --json`, sizes only a line with no item estimate (or a `pr` line) for that day with `kf estimate size`, and takes overlap and budget from `kf estimate overlap` and `kf estimate stats`. It runs no script of its own, appends nothing to `.kf/travel/estimates.md`, and reads the measurement with `kf metrics`.

## Use cases
- UC-001 Estimate an item in its contract and approve it — `kf approve` records `meta.estimate` and the `approved` record carries it; a missing or XL estimate is a planning notice, never a refusal.
- UC-002 Measure an item at its close — entering review records `filesChanged`, the close writes `metrics.json` and the loop-metrics row, and `kf metrics` reads it; a failed measurement only warns.
- UC-003 Read the harness estimate and calibration — `travel` and other readers use `kf estimate size|stats|overlap|show` and never estimate an item that has a recorded estimate.
